Corn

Zea mays

Corn grows from a seed planted in warm, moist soil. As it sprouts, the young plant sends down roots and grows upward into a tall stalk with long green leaves. Over time, the plant forms tassels at the top and ears along the stalk, where kernels develop and ripen. With enough sunlight, water, and space, corn continues to grow until it is ready to harvest.

Corn originated in southern Mexico about 9,000 years ago from a wild grass called teosinte, which Indigenous farmers selectively bred over many generations into the crop we know today. It spread across North and South America long before Europeans arrived and became a major food source for civilizations like the Maya, Aztec, and Inca. After Christopher Columbus brought corn back to Europe in the late 1400s, it quickly spread to Africa and Asia. Today, corn is one of the world’s most important crops, used for food, animal feed, and industrial products. Its history shows how human selection turned a small wild grass into a global staple.

Corn is used by people, livestock, and many industries. People eat it directly in foods like corn on the cob, popcorn, tortillas, cereal, corn syrup, and cornstarch. Farmers also use a large share of corn to feed cattle, pigs, chickens, and other animals that provide meat, milk, and eggs. It is also turned into ethanol fuel and other industrial products such as starch, alcohols, soaps, inks, and bioplastics. In short, corn is used in everyday food, animal feed, fuel, and manufacturing.
